About

Computational biology meets machine learning

I am a Bioinformatics Scientist in the Aparicio Lab at BC Cancer Research Centre, where I develop deep learning frameworks and scalable pipelines for cancer genomics. My work spans ctDNA deconvolution, six-letter methylation sequencing, chromatin profiling (CUT&Tag), and DLP+ single-cell copy number analysis.

I hold an M.Sc. in Bioinformatics from the University of British Columbia (GPA 95.3/100) and a B.Sc. in Computer Engineering from Amirkabir University of Technology.

Outside the lab I mentor the next generation of computational biology researchers at the UBC National Canadian Medical Datathon (2025 & 2026).

Research

Current work at the Aparicio Lab

My research focuses on building machine learning tools that make sense of large-scale genomic and epigenomic data in the context of cancer biology.

⬡

ctDNA Deconvolution

Developing a framework to deconvolve circulating tumor DNA from plasma methylation data to infer tissue-of-origin and tumor fraction for minimal residual disease detection.

MethylationDeep LearningcfDNA
◈

CUT&Tag Signal Modeling

Building end-to-end pipelines using Enformer and Borzoi transformer architectures for large-scale chromatin profiling and regulatory element characterization.

EnformerBorzoiEpigenomics
◇

Six-Letter Methylation Sequencing

Designing 5mC/hmC-aware feature extraction methods for six-letter (Biomodal) methylation sequencing, enabling epigenomic characterization at single-base resolution.

5mC/hmCBiomodalEpigenetics
○

DLP+ Single-Cell Copy Number

Analyzing DLP+ (Direct Library Preparation Plus) single-cell whole-genome sequencing data from CX5461-treated cells to identify copy number–based predictors of drug response.

DLP+scDNA-seqDrug Response
△

G-Quadruplex Characterization

Developing deep learning models to characterize G-quadruplex regulatory structures and their role in genome stability and gene regulation.

Regulatory GenomicsDeep Learning
□

Mutational Signature Analysis

HDP-based mutational signature decomposition and SBS signature analysis of nanopore sequencing data to identify mutational patterns at single-nucleotide resolution.

SignaturesNanoporeHDP
